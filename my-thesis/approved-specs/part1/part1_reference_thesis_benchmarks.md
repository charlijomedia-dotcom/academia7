# Part 1: Structural comparison of four economics dissertations

**Project:** Revision of the dissertation described in the project brief as *Forecasting Macroeconomic Aggregates under Economic Instability: Theory, Nonlinearity, and Policy Implications*.

**Date:** 3 October 2026  
**Status:** APPROVED
**Companion:** `part1_thesis_structure_principles.md`  
**Intended repository location after review:** `approved-specs/part1/`

## 1. Main finding

There is no single chapter structure shared by these four dissertations. Their strongest transferable feature is that the reader can follow why a problem requires a particular analysis and how the analysis supports the answer.

For the user's intended monograph, Shiller supplies the closest overall progression. Del Campo supplies a useful general introduction and concluding synthesis. Akerlof shows how one unresolved question can motivate the next chapter. Bernanke shows how to start with an economic mechanism and state a contribution early.

This combination supports a coherent monograph. It does not justify turning the dissertation into three separate papers. Nor does it establish that adopting a particular structure will produce an award-winning thesis.

### Recognition is not a structural criterion

The working filename retains the earlier phrase “award winning” for continuity. It should not be read as a verified classification of all four dissertations.

Bernanke, Shiller, and Akerlof subsequently received the economics prize in memory of Alfred Nobel, in 2022, 2013, and 2001 respectively. The official prize records establish recognition of their research, not an award for these dissertation manuscripts. A specific dissertation prize for Del Campo was not verified in this review. His thesis remains useful as a supplied French doctoral benchmark.

The sample was selected by the user. It has no comparison group of unsuccessful theses. Consequently, it cannot establish that the observed structures caused later scholarly success.

## 3. Comparative overview

| Dimension | Bernanke | Shiller | Akerlof | Del Campo |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Overall organization | Three explicitly “loosely connected” essays | Six chapters advancing one model and its empirical implementation | Four related essays with a shared macroeconomic concern | General introduction, three research chapters, general conclusion |
| Opening strategy | Economic adjustment problem and mechanism | Focused literature gap and testable model | Abstract connects the essays; individual openings state a problem or contradiction | Environmental problem, key concepts, explicit questions, outline and results |
| Role of theory | Main contribution within each essay | Generates relationships to be estimated and tested | Defines mechanisms and their implications | Establishes welfare criteria, constraints, and conditional implications |
| Method placement | Within the relevant essay | Dedicated estimator chapters, justified by an estimator contribution | Formal models and proofs within essays | Frameworks within chapters; several technical details in chapter appendices |
| Empirical role | Predominantly theoretical; empirical importance sometimes left open | Large empirical chapter applying the preceding model and estimator | Predominantly theoretical, with a small empirical check near the end of essay 3 | Primarily theoretical, with numerical and graphical illustrations |
| Closing strategy | Essay-level conclusions or closing interpretation; no separate general conclusion in the contents | Final chapter examines weaknesses and refinements; no separate general conclusion in the contents | Essay-level closure; no separate general concluding chapter in this scan | Chapter conclusions plus general summary and explicit limits |
| Best transfer | Start with the mechanism and bounded contribution | Make dependencies between chapters explicit | Let each new question emerge from a preceding limitation | Explain the whole project and synthesize its conditional findings |
| Main mismatch | Explicit essay format | One chapter prepared as a standalone paper; considerable technical detail | Essay format and uneven presentation | Three research blocks; French institution alone does not make it the exact monograph format requested |

These differences are substantive. A theoretical dissertation need not have the same structure as an empirical forecasting dissertation. Numerical illustrations of a theoretical model are not substitutes for out-of-sample forecast evidence.

## 4. Bernanke: begin with an economic mechanism

### 4.1 Observed architecture

The abstract states that the dissertation contains three loosely connected theoretical essays on long-term commitments. Their common purpose is to connect individual decision problems with macroeconomic phenomena, especially the business cycle (Bernanke, 1979, PDF pp. 2–3).

| Block | PDF pages | Function |
|---|---:|---|
| Front matter and contents | 1–6 | State the common theme and summarize each essay |
| Chapter 1: timing of irreversible investments | 7–70 | Study investment timing when information arrives and commitments are costly to reverse |
| Chapter 2: efficient excess capacity and unemployment | 71–101 | Examine why temporarily idle resources can arise under dynamic efficiency |
| Chapter 3: incomplete labor contracts | 102–145 | Use contractual restrictions to explain employment outcomes |
| General bibliography | 146–150 | Collect references |

The lengths are unequal because the arguments require different amounts of development. The contents do not impose symmetrical chapters.

### 4.2 How the opening works

Chapter 1 begins with an economic tension: flexible adjustment may be a poor approximation over business-cycle horizons. Irreversible commitments under incomplete information then explain why waiting can be rational. The discussion narrows to the investment problem before introducing the formal machinery (PDF pp. 8–11).

The opening also gives a functional roadmap. It identifies the decision problem, a tractable case, a nonstationary extension, an application, and the conclusion. Each section has a purpose in the argument. It is not merely a list of headings.

Formal assumptions follow with explanations of what they accomplish. For example, the wealth assumption both simplifies the savings problem and restricts investible resources (PDF p. 12). This is a useful model for explaining why an assumption is present.

### 4.3 Results, conclusions, and limits

The first essay's conclusion distinguishes its mechanism from the broader conjecture about investment fluctuations and identifies extensions (PDF pp. 64–65). Chapter 2 explicitly leaves the empirical importance of its mechanism unresolved: other explanations might dominate in actual data (PDF p. 101).

This distinction is valuable for the forecasting thesis. Showing that a mechanism could generate nonlinear dynamics does not establish that a nonlinear model forecasts better. Those are separate claims requiring separate evidence.

### 4.4 What transfers

- Open a substantive section with the economic difficulty it addresses.
- Define the key mechanism before giving its equations.
- Explain the role of important assumptions.
- State what the analysis establishes and what remains conjectural.
- Let chapter length follow the burden of the argument.

### 4.5 What should not transfer

Do not adopt the three-essay organization or omit a general synthesis simply because Bernanke did so. Do not copy the historical bibliography or typography. The user's monograph requires stronger links between chapters than the thesis's own description of “loosely connected” essays promises.

## 5. Shiller: connect the model, estimator, and evidence

### 5.1 Observed architecture

The abstract identifies the theoretical model, empirical implementation, estimation contribution, and refinements. It tells the reader what the dissertation does before the detailed mathematics begins (Shiller, 1972, PDF p. 2).

| Block | PDF pages | Function |
|---|---:|---|
| Front matter and lists | 1–7 | Preview the project and locate tables and figures |
| Chapter 1: a model of the term structure | 8–18 | State the model, its relevance, and the research program |
| Chapter 2: mathematical development | 19–63 | Derive relationships and extensions needed for analysis |
| Chapter 3: distributed-lag estimator from smoothness priors | 64–85 | Develop and illustrate an estimation approach |
| Chapter 4: further estimator issues and theorems | 86–98 | Establish additional properties and relationships |
| Chapter 5: estimation results | 99–164 | Apply the method to the relationships developed earlier |
| Chapter 6: additional refinements | 165–181 | Examine weaknesses and features omitted from the initial model |
| Biographical note | 182 | Administrative end matter |

The table of contents gives subsection page starts. Some differ from chapter opening pages. For example, the estimator chapter begins on PDF p. 64 although its first listed subsection begins on p. 65.

### 5.2 Why this is the closest overall benchmark

The first chapter identifies a specific opportunity within existing term-structure research. Its model has implications that can be compared with observed data. The purpose of the mathematical development is therefore visible early (PDF pp. 8–17).

Chapter 5 explicitly says that the estimator developed in the preceding chapters will estimate the relationships developed in Chapter 2. The empirical work is introduced as a test of consistency with the theory (PDF p. 99). This sentence makes the dependency between theory, method, and evidence unusually clear.

The transferable sequence is:

1. State an economic problem and a precise analytical claim.
2. Define the model and derive what must be examined.
3. Explain why the estimation procedure can answer that question.
4. Present the evidence.
5. Examine where simplifying assumptions may explain discrepancies.

This is a recommended logical sequence, not a requirement to use six chapters.

### 5.3 Why a large methods section is justified here

Shiller develops an estimator. Chapter 3 is explicitly described as a self-contained paper prepared for publication (PDF p. 16). Chapter 4 then develops further properties. These chapters therefore carry part of the original contribution rather than simply describing standard tools.

For the user's thesis, this creates a decision rule: a lengthy methodological discussion needs a demonstrated role in the contribution or in the reader's ability to assess the evidence. A catalogue of familiar models does not gain the same justification merely by resembling Shiller's chapter titles.

### 5.4 How the evidence is organized

The empirical chapter begins with the comparison being made, then defines data and implementation choices (PDF pp. 99–103). Results are connected to numbered tables and figures. Detailed tables include coefficients, fitted or forecast values, and underlying data, rather than only favorable headline measures (PDF pp. 114–163).

Shiller also explains how changes in the sample can account for a change in fit, rather than automatically attributing it to a different estimator (PDF p. 105). This supports clear separation of changes in method from changes in the comparison sample.

The final chapter begins with weaknesses of the model. Tax treatment, bond maturity, and call provisions are considered because they may explain discrepancies in the preceding evidence (PDF p. 165). Refinements are motivated by a specific limitation.

### 5.5 What transfers, and what needs updating

Transfer the explicit connection between chapters, the justification for method choice, and the movement from results to limitations. Keep the decisive model definitions and evaluation design in the main text. Put repetitive output and supplementary derivations in appendices when that improves reading.

Do not treat the historical forecast exercises or fit statistics as a ready-made modern evaluation protocol. Part 5 must separately establish the information available at each forecast origin, data vintage policy, estimation windows, horizons, and comparison tests. The historical manuscript cannot settle those choices for this project.

Also, this benchmark does not support a blanket rule that results must appear early in page count. Its principal empirical chapter begins on p. 99. Its strength is that the reader understands why the preceding work is needed.

## 6. Akerlof: make the next question necessary

### 6.1 Observed architecture

The abstract presents a connected sequence of essays. It moves from capital and marginal products to wage determination and unemployment, then to investment and aggregate demand, and finally to the role of money (Akerlof, 1966, PDF pp. 3–4).

The scan does not supply a conventional consolidated contents page in its front matter. The map below is reconstructed from the essay openings, not copied from an invented table of contents.

| Block | PDF pages | Function |
|---|---:|---|
| Front matter | 1–5 | Identify the thesis, explain its essays, and acknowledge contributions |
| Stability, Marginal Products, Putty and Clay | 6–33 | Establish model properties, their interpretation, and proofs |
| Wages, Capital and Unemployment | 34–44 | Connect technology and wage determination with employment |
| Income, Investment, and Wages | 45–84 | Examine competing wage, investment, and aggregate-demand arguments |
| An Essay on the Theory of Money | 85–115 | Connect monetary mechanisms with the preceding macroeconomic concerns |
| Biographical notes | 116 | Administrative end matter |

### 6.2 The linking device

The abstract explains why later essays follow earlier ones. A change in distribution raises the wage question. The Keynesian framework then raises the role of money. This is a stronger connection than saying that several chapters all concern macroeconomics (PDF pp. 3–4).

The second essay carries forward the putty-clay technology of Chapter I and explains its employment mechanism in words before presenting the model (PDF pp. 34–35). The third opens with a tension between familiar economic propositions and sets out a model in which different effects can occur (PDF pp. 45–46).

For the user's monograph, the lesson is to write genuine transitions. A chapter on economic instability should identify what the empirical design must distinguish. A design chapter should identify the evidence needed. Results should then show which questions are resolved and which remain open.

### 6.3 Technical clarity and supporting material

The first essay announces the proposition and outlines the proof strategy before detailed algebra (PDF p. 11). It also discusses how restrictive assumptions affect the result (PDF pp. 23–24). Technical appendices follow within that essay (PDF pp. 25–33).

This suggests a useful three-step exposition: explain the intuition, state the formal object, then supply the derivation or an appendix reference. It does not justify removing definitions or assumptions from the main text.

### 6.4 Evidence that must not be hidden

Near the end of the third essay, Akerlof reports a small empirical exercise and describes its results as inconclusive before discussing their implications (PDF pp. 83–84). The structural lesson is to display difficult evidence. The statistical interpretation itself should not be copied as a present-day standard: low fit or a nonsignificant estimate alone does not prove independence or establish a competing model.

### 6.5 What should not transfer

Do not copy the essay packaging, restarted pagination, uneven chapter lengths as a rule, or rhetorical language. The user's objective tone and common notation should apply throughout the revised monograph. Where new work is collaborative, acknowledge contributions accurately rather than implying sole authorship.

## 7. Del Campo: frame the whole dissertation and synthesize it

### 7.1 Observed architecture

Del Campo provides the clearest general introduction and general conclusion in this sample. The contents distinguish context, concepts, research questions, and an outline that includes results (Del Campo, 2018, PDF pp. 9–11).

| Block | PDF pages | Function |
|---|---:|---|
| Front matter | 1–12 | Title pages, acknowledgments, abstract, and contents |
| General introduction | 13–40 | Establish concepts, connect them, state questions, and preview findings |
| Chapter 1 | 41–86 | Study sustaining welfare when intratemporal inequalities matter, with appendices |
| Chapter 2 | 87–112 | Study distribution during growth toward the golden rule, with an appendix |
| Chapter 3 | 113–144 | Study resource transfers and sustainability, with appendices |
| General conclusion | 145–150 | Synthesize results, limits, and prospects |
| Bibliography | 151–169 | References supporting the dissertation |

The contents list the bibliography at printed p. 157. Direct inspection shows that it begins at PDF p. 151, printed p. 139. The map uses the actual PDF sequence rather than the erroneous contents entry.

### 7.2 How the introduction builds a research problem

The opening uses an environmental-resource problem to motivate the interaction of claims within and across generations (PDF p. 13). The introduction then explains the two dimensions of equity and how they connect. Explicit questions appear in section 0.5, followed by a substantive account of the chapters and findings in section 0.6 (PDF pp. 35–40).

The roadmap explains both the purpose and results of each chapter. It also states how restrictions in earlier chapters motivate the next step. The absence of transfers in the first two chapters helps motivate the third (PDF pp. 38–40).

This is useful for the user's thesis: the introduction should explain the project as a whole and preview verified findings in the final manuscript. Before empirical results exist, the preview must remain a description of planned analyses.

### 7.3 How chapters and appendices divide the work

Each substantive chapter has an introduction, model or framework, analysis, and conclusion. Supporting proofs, limiting cases, and further illustrations appear in chapter appendices (contents, PDF pp. 9–11; example appendix, PDF pp. 141–143).

These are primarily theoretical results and illustrations. Their organization can inform exposition, but it cannot replace the distinct requirements of an empirical forecasting exercise.

### 7.4 What the conclusion adds

The general conclusion restates the common framework and explains how the three chapters change assumptions and address related questions (PDF pp. 145–148). It then identifies limitations stemming from both modeling choices and deliberate omissions (PDF pp. 148–150).

The final discussion presents recommendations as conditional on the framework. For the user's thesis, the corresponding principle is to distinguish forecast evidence from claims about the effects of policy interventions. The latter require support beyond a model's forecast ranking.

### 7.5 Correcting the earlier characterization

A French doctorate is not automatically the specific monograph form requested by the user. Del Campo has three substantive research blocks and includes material associated with prior collaborative work, as acknowledged in the first chapter's conclusion (PDF p. 72). It is therefore best used here for its framing, integration, and synthesis. It is not evidence that the user's dissertation should become a three-paper thesis.

## 8. Length discipline: what the files actually show

The four PDFs contain 116, 150, 169, and 182 pages. These totals include different combinations of front matter, appendices, references, tables, and administrative pages. They do not measure comparable amounts of prose.

Several concrete observations prevent misleading conclusions:

- Shiller devotes 66 PDF pages to the empirical chapter, including many detailed output pages.
- Del Campo devotes 28 PDF pages to the general introduction, which includes conceptual and literature discussion.
- Bernanke's three research blocks are about 64, 31, and 44 PDF pages long.
- Akerlof's essays differ sharply in length and restart their pagination.

Thus, “short thesis” should not become an arbitrary page ceiling. The useful rule is to remove material that does not help define, justify, test, interpret, or qualify the contribution. University requirements and the actual empirical burden take priority over a benchmark's page count.

## 9. Transfer matrix for the forecasting monograph

These are structural recommendations. They do not approve any variable, model, horizon, test, or substantive conclusion.

| Observed practice | Source anchor | Recommended transfer | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Economic tension precedes machinery | Bernanke, PDF pp. 8–12 | Start with the forecasting problem created by instability | Do not assert that instability guarantees nonlinear forecast gains |
| Model and evidence have an explicit connection | Shiller, PDF p. 99 | Explain what each analysis can establish | Do not present model fit as automatic out-of-sample success |
| A methods chapter carries a contribution | Shiller, PDF pp. 16, 64–86 | Allocate methods space according to scientific need | Do not imitate the length for standard tool descriptions |
| A later question follows an earlier limitation | Akerlof, PDF pp. 3–4; Del Campo, PDF pp. 38–40 | Give each transition a reason | Do not invent dependencies between unrelated analyses |
| Intuition precedes a detailed proof | Akerlof, PDF p. 11 | Explain the statistical or economic purpose before equations | Keep necessary definitions and conditions explicit |
| Unresolved evidence remains visible | Bernanke, PDF p. 101; Akerlof, PDF pp. 83–84 | Report limits and mixed findings | Do not infer equivalence from nonsignificance alone |
| Refinements answer specific weaknesses | Shiller, PDF p. 165 | Organize robustness around threats to the main conclusion | Do not select specifications because they improve the desired result |
| Whole-project synthesis | Del Campo, PDF pp. 145–150 | End with an integrated answer and its boundaries | Do not add new results in the conclusion |

## 10. Implications for the next parts

Part 1 establishes a set of design principles, not a final thesis outline. No claims about the exact defects of the submitted May 2026 manuscript are made on the basis of the benchmark PDFs alone.

Part 2 must read the original jury reports and register their requirements. Part 3 must read the submitted dissertation and map its actual argument, methods, and evidence. Part 4 can then assign chapters and sections by combining those findings with this benchmark analysis. Part 5 must turn the chosen empirical approach into an executable specification.

The proposed monograph should make one chain of reasoning visible: economic problem, precise question, relevant mechanisms, empirical design, evidence, challenges to that evidence, and qualified interpretation. This is a synthesis drawn from the comparison. It is not the literal table of contents shared by the four authors.
