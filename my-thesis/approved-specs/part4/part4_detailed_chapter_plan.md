## 1. Formal architecture required by Prof. Verne

The thesis uses **two Parts, each containing two substantive chapters**.

| Part | Scientific task | First chapter task | Second chapter task |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Part I: From Macroeconomic Instability to a Testable Forecasting Problem** | Formulate and identify the problem | Explain the economic mechanisms and literature gap | Build the fair empirical test |
| **Part II: Testing the Forecast Value of Nonlinear State Dependence** | Test and challenge the answer | Establish fit and baseline forecast evidence | Try to invalidate the findings and interpret what survives |

This preserves the four scientific functions identified in the first Part 4 draft while restoring the two-part structure explicitly requested by the advisor.

**Results-order rule:**

1. Are the data and specification eligible?
2. Does the model fit observed data credibly?
3. Do residual/model diagnostics support its use?
4. Does it forecast better out of sample?
5. Is the gain statistically and economically meaningful?
6. Does it help in expansions, recessions, peaks, or troughs?
7. What is the narrowest defensible baseline conclusion?

**Required concluding section:**  
**3.9 Chapter 3 Concluding Synthesis: Where nonlinear complexity earns its place, if anywhere**

Length: approximately one page.  
Function: answer the Chapter 3 question, distinguish fit from forecast superiority, report failures, and list the claims Chapter 4 must challenge.

## 5. General Conclusion

**Working title:** *What Nonlinear Forecasting Can and Cannot Contribute to Macroeconomic Policy Under Instability*

The General Conclusion must synthesize the theoretical and empirical results and answer the central problématique directly. It must not introduce new evidence.

## 6. Chapter-specific synthesis rule

Every substantive chapter ends with a **chapter-specific one-page concluding synthesis**, not a generic summary.

Each synthesis must:

1. restate that chapter's exact question;
2. summarize only what that chapter established;
3. state the chapter's main takeaway and qualification;
4. identify the unresolved issue that makes the next chapter necessary;
5. introduce no new citation-driven literature, test, empirical result, or argument.

This requirement is mandatory for Part 9.
