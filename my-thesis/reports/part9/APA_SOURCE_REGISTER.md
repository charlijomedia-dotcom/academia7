## 1. Verification and citation rules

1. **Scope of this register:** 61 source records: the original 49 plus 12 Chapter 1 additions, spanning journal research, books, scripture and official institutional publications. Bibliographic metadata and relevance have been checked against the publisher, institutional source or trusted scholarly indexing shown on each record, as of 12 November 2026. A linked record does **not** mean its full text has been read cover-to-cover.
2. **APA 7 only:** Use exactly the listed author/date/title/volume/issue/page/DOI conventions in the working bibliography; check the exact edition and quotations again when drafting. Use title/year convention for the biblical electronic edition, with explicit book/chapter/verse locator.
3. **Q1:** The Part 5 audit prioritizes Q1 Scopus/SCImago venues. This register **does not certify the 2026 quartile of every journal separately**; bibliographic verification and indexing/quartile verification are different tasks. The scripture, books and institutional reports are not journal articles and need no Q1 label.
4. **Intellectual timing:** Papers published before the thesis's November 2026 writing stage belong to prior literature, even if from 2026. Results generated by this thesis remain **our own evidence**, not outside literature. Do not overstate novelty of nonlinear forecasting.
5. **Causal boundary:** Evidence that forecasts were wrong, and evidence that households faced hardship, **do not** alone show that the forecast error caused the hardship or that a better forecast would have led to an optimal policy.
6. **Data-vintage boundary:** Part 8 uses a single frozen historical-vintage dataset in pseudo-out-of-sample evaluation, not authentic sequence-of-real-time-vintage forecasting. Do not describe it as real-time policy evaluation.
7. **No cherry-picking:** For each source, its *supported use* and *prohibited inference* below must both be respected. Prior findings favorable and unfavorable to nonlinear methods must be reviewed together.

## 2. Introductory evidence and the Joseph opening

**Safe opening statement:** Genesis 41 in the Douay–Rheims Challoner revision *narrates* Joseph's prediction of seven years of plenty followed by seven years of famine, his proposal to reserve one-fifth of harvest during plenty, and the subsequent use of stored grain. It illustrates how a forecast may inform precautionary public stocks. **It does not prove** the episode is the first ever implemented macroeconomic policy, or establish the narrative as independent historical data. Cite `S01` with `Genesis 41:25–36, 41:53–57`.

**Recommended evidence chain:** `S01` (narrative) → `S02` (63-country recession forecast misses) → `S03` (Bank of England inflation forecast RMSE rising 0.64 to 4.60 percentage points) → `S04` (UK borrowing forecast £85bn vs £152bn outturn, published difference £66bn due to rounding) → `S05` (real-wage hardship, 30/34 countries) → `S06` (real-time informational policy challenge). Each item proves a *different* statement; do not conflate forecast error, adverse outcome and causal effect.

**Edition note:** Project Gutenberg catalogue `#1581` gives release **1998** and later metadata update **2023**; the reference uses the electronic edition release year, **not** the metadata update as if a new scholarly edition. Challoner revised the historic translation in 1749–1752. Check with supervisor whether a required style guide prefers an original/revision-year parenthetical for ancient/classical religious work; keep APA author/date conventions consistent.
